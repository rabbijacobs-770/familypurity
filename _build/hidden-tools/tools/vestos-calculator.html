Your custom
At what time?

How it works

  1. Monthly cycle (yom hachodesh): the same Hebrew date next month, in the same half of the day.
  2. Average cycle (onah beinonis): the 30th day, for a full day. Many communities also keep the 31st day.
  3. Interval cycle (veses haflagah): the same interval as last time. In the Chabad custom it is counted from the hefsek tahara; others count from one onset to the next.
  4. On each date an examination is made from the hour the last period began. Only actual menstruation counts, not stains. Times, Chabad custom · Major customs
Half a day or a full day?

The monthly and interval dates are a half day: sunset to sunrise, or sunrise to sunset. The average cycle is a full day, from one sunset to the next.

Common questions

What is an onah?

A half day: daytime from sunrise to sunset, or nighttime from sunset to sunrise.

Why does it ask what time the period began?

After sunset the Hebrew date has already moved to the next day, and the separation is kept in the same half of the day.

Do stains count when calculating?

No. Only actual menstruation, or blood found through an examination, is recorded for these dates.

What about a fixed cycle (veset kavua)?

This calculator covers the common case. If your cycle follows a fixed pattern, ask Rabbi Jacobs.
