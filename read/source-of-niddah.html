Source of Niddah

A man that lies with a woman who had her [menstrual] flow... as she had revealed the source of her blood. Leviticus 20:18

Uterine Bleeding

A woman becomes a “menstruant,” niddah in Hebrew, when blood leaves the womb1Even if it hasn’t exited the body, Mehaber 183:1.. This is based on the verse above, “she had revealed the source of her blood2Leviticus 20:18..” The source refers to the womb3Throughout, womb and uterus are used interchangeably., where the infant is formed and it is from here that menstrual (niddah) blood comes4Rambam, Hilchos Isoorei Biah 5:3; Shach, Shulchan Aruch Admur Hazoken 183:1; Bodei Hashulchan 183:3. “Only uterine or cervical bleeding renders a woman a niddah (with the exception of hymenal bleeding,” Gefen Poriah 1:14. “Scripturally, the external os, cervix and the womb itself are collectively called ‘the womb.’ Only blood issuing from these cause niddah (or zavah). Blood which definitely issues from any other reproductive organ is not, except hymenal blooding, which was rabbinically prohibited,” Gefen Poriah Chapter 1, endnote 48. Bleeding or staining which may be traced or dismissed to other sources should be decided by an orthodox rabbi who is knowledgeable in these matters. Some examples: injuries, chemical irritations e.g. douches, contraceptive material, urinary tract, kidneys, ovaries, fallopian tubes, vaginal..

A woman enters this niddah state whenever blood leaves the womb regardless of the cause5Taz 183:1. and whether for natural reasons e.g. menstruation, spotting, giving birth6See Chapter Fifteen., emotional excitement, physical exertion7Mehaber, Taz 183:1, Shulchan Aruch Admur Hazoken 183:2. or because of medication8Nishmat Avrohom 183:3, Chosen Yeshuot 1:1., or an internal gynecological examination which involved penetration into the uterus9Aruch Hashulchan Section 43, cited in Nishmat Avrahom 183:2. In these instances, a Rabbi who is knowledgeable in these matters should be consulted to determine the wife’s status. See additional examples in section Gynecological Considerations and Appendix - Gynecology..

Even a minuscule amount of blood exiting the uterus causes the state10Niddah 66:a. Mehaber 183:3, Shach 183:3, Shulchan Aruch Admur Hazoken 183:3., regardless of whether it is moist or dry11Mehaber 188:4, Shulchan Aruch Admur Hazoken 188:16.—even if there was no sensation of menstrual bleeding or opening of the uterus accompanying the appearance of blood12Shulchan Aruch Admur Hazoken 183:3, Shach 183:2..

Niddah Before Marriage

This state of niddah, as well as the spiritual excision of both13Leviticus 18:19, 20:18, see Bodei Hashulchan 183:11, 183:13 for having relations at this time is applicable even before marriage14Ramo 183:1., even if there wasn’t full penetration15Darchei Teshuva 183:14. and there was no emission of semen16Bodei Hashulchan 183:11..

The other prohibitions associated with niddah, including kissing, hugging, and affectionate contact17E.g. handshaking, add more: Research, also apply to an unmarried woman18Bodei Hashulchan 183:12, Darchei Teshuva 183:15., since an unmarried woman has never left her niddah state by immersing in a mikveh.

Additionally it is forbidden to be alone with them in closed quarters (unlike one’s wife when she is a niddah—see Chapter Four).

There is no minimum age for a woman to become niddah19Even “on the day she is born,” Rambam, Isorei Bia 4:1, Darchei Teshuva 183:1. Rashi on Leviticus 15:19.. Even a girl who has begun menstruating20Or otherwise become a niddah. Regarding niddah resulting from a stain, kesem, before age twelve see Mehaber 190:2. is in a constant niddah state until she immerses herself in a for the first time before her wedding21Immersion by single women, whether unmarried, divorced or widowed, except in preparation for marriage is forbidden, Beis Yosef end of Chapter 183, Gefen Porioh 14:10. A Talmudic discussion revolves around whether it is a mitzvah for someone who is ritually impure to seek purification as soon as possible. The Mehaber (192:7) holds that it is not a mitzvah for a menstruant to immerse as soon as possible (except when fulfilling “be fruitful and multiply” or her marital obligation for intimacy). Therefore, there is no mitzvah for a widow or bachelorette to purify themselves from niddah, Bodei Hashulchan 197:10..

Menstruation

Menstrual bleeding appearing as it normally does, (even when the sensation of blood flowing was unnoticed), as well as when discovered through an internal examination without noticeable physical sensations, render a woman niddah. From a halachic standpoint, it is considered that the physical sensation was slight and therefore went unnoticed22Pischei Magidim 1:14..

Questionably Originated Elsewhere. If there is reason to believe that staining originated somewhere other than the womb, for example: vagina, urinary canal, bladder or an injury, cut, bruise, infection, or medical irritations, hemorrhoids, etc., an orthodox Rabbi who is knowledgable in these matters should be consulted to determine her status.

He may base his decision partly on medical evidence. Nonetheless it is a difficult subject which can only be decided through a competent authority.

Discolored Urine

If she notices blood after urinating, an orthodox Rabbi who is knowledgable in these matters should be consulted.

Facts which might influence his decision32These are found in the Ramo 191:1. Within these situations are interwoven numerous and differing halachic opinions too complex to narrow into one final decision in the text. include: whether the blood was found in the water or on the seat, if she sat over the middle of the toilet seat or on its edge, if this is a one time experience or it repeats itself, if she felt pain or burning during urination, and if she ate some red food, such as beets, prior to this.

Gynecological Considerations

Opening of the Uterus. The sages33Niddah 21:a, b. asserted, “There can be no opening of the uterus34Rashi Niddah 21:a. without blood flowing35Mehaber 188:3, Ramo 194:2. This rule applies primarily to the internal os, but also applies to the external os as a stringency, Shiurei Shevet Halevy 188:3, 4..”

This applies not only when something is expelled, such as during menstruation, but also when something is inserted36Shulchan Aruch Admur Hazoken 188:8. This applies for pregnant, nursing, and postmenopausal women, as well, Pischei Teshuva 194:4. Some poskim hold this rule does not apply when something is inserted into the uterus, see Appendix - Gynecology for more deliberations. into the uterus, e.g. a medical instrument.

Some internal gynecological examinations involve penetration into the uterus using various types of medical instruments37For example: an endometrial biopsy with a biopsy curet or a hysteroscopy (visual examination of the uterine cavity through an instrument called a hysteroscope). A number of instruments are used to perform a cervical biopsy, for example: Tischler cervical forceps, Kevorkian-Young forceps, and the Duncan endometrial curet.. When the uterus is penetrated with a medical instrument, the niddah state almost invariably results38See Appendix - Gynecological., even when no sign of blood is present.
