Absence Makes the Heart Grow Fonder (Harchakos)

By Rabbi Fishel Jacobs · Last updated October 23, 2026

The Torah separated the husband and wife during the time of her impurity in order to make her as desirable to him as the day of their wedding. Niddah 31:b

The idea of no physical contact for at least twelve days a month is perhaps the one aspect within the Jewish marriage cycle which has most captured the public’s attention. In fact, half of the month, except during pregnancy, or after menopause, we aren’t allowed physical contact. Other areas of our behavior are limited as well.

Newlyweds

If you are a couple preparing for marriage, you may have mixed emotions regarding this subject. You are anxiously preparing for marriage. The most marked difference between prior to and following the wedding night is that afterwards physical contact is allowed. This itself entails a sense of excitement. Yet along with this comes the abrupt awareness that during married life this will be limited to only half of any given month except, of course, in the case of pregnancy. Experience has shown that people become accustomed to these days. What may have seemed a long time at the beginning of marriage adopts much smaller proportions later on.

Review following Childbirth

During pregnancy menstrual bleeding ceases. During these months the rules governing separation are not in force (except in special cases involving spotting and the like). Therefore it is very important that following childbirth the couple review these laws.

Studying Together

Studying the laws of separation together1Not from the same book, Shiurei Shevet HaLevy on Mehaber 195:2. is, for a number of reasons, recommended. These laws, in particular, are addressed to both the wife and the husband. Jointly reviewing them will ensure that their knowledge is on par. This can reduce misunderstanding and hurt feelings. Misunderstandings on details in law can be cleared up together, leaving less room for mistakes.

To the Wife

During this time, you should attempt to lighten your husband’s responsibility by dressing appropriately. This is not the time to dress up. Although you needn’t dress unattractively you should bear in mind the effect your attire has on your husband and act accordingly.

Perfume should be reduced or eliminated if possible.

For those conscious of fashion or their contemporaries’ opinions, this may be a personal trial. But conscious effort should be invested in helping your husband wait for the time when you will again be permitted to him.

To the Husband

You should be aware of your wife’s feelings during this time. It sometimes happens that the wife feels “at fault” for the present situation. You should try to negate that feeling or at least not nurture it. The forbidden times are designed by the Al-mighty as part of the marriage cycle.

To Husband and Wife

The times when physical contact is forbidden should be seen as essentially a time of replenishing. Any experience, when available on a constant basis, becomes bland. The best summer vacation will, after time, drag on, the newest car becomes uninteresting and taken for granted, and marriage is no different. A famous Rabbi once wrote that the on-again off-again nature of the Jewish couple is, in fact, the cementing element ensuring a refreshing, wholesome atmosphere between the couple—“as a newlywed couple on a monthly basis2Siddur Beis Yakov..”

Not only is there a benefit following the forbidden time, but also during this time one can strive to relate on a higher plane. The absence of physical relations is a perfect opportunity for increased verbal communication. This is a time when many couples rediscover deeper levels of communication, working out family problems and the like.

The laws of separation are in force whether in the days of menstruation or in the seven white days and there is no difference whether she is a niddah due to having a period or finding a stain.

Shulchan Aruch 195:14

Background

The Talmudic Rabbis forbade being alone with members of the opposite sex with whom marital relations are forbidden and the punishment for willing transgression, i.e., intercourse, is excision; for example, one’s daughter-in-law or sister-in-law. This prohibition, called yichud, was designed to ensure modest and proper behavior3Note: yichud with any woman is likewise forbidden, except a man with his daughter and a woman with her son, Kitzur Shulchan Aruch 152:1..

Relations with one’s spouse during the time of are forbidden and the scriptural punishment is excision. Yichud should, therefore, have fallen into their legislation. However, due to practical considerations and obvious limitations, the prohibition of yichud was not extended to one’s wife.

Since the Rabbis allowed this closeness between husband and wife as an exception to the above rule, they saw the necessity to limit contact in a wide spectrum of other areas. These areas are explained in this chapter.

For a husband and wife to be alone, yichud, while the wife is niddah, two conditions must have been met. First they must have had completed marital relations at least once and second, there must be a time when she will be permissible to him again. The reason is that since they have already had relations and the present niddah state is only temporary, the couple will tend to be more relaxed and will more easily refrain from forbidden relations4Tzemach Tzedek 195:1.. An example where the first condition is lacking: they married while the bride was a niddah5Or she became niddah before they had marital relations, Mehaber 192:4.. An example of the second condition lacking: the wife having a skin disease which precluded her going to the mikveh.

General

The laws of separation are identical regardless of whether the reason for the wife’s present state of niddah is biblical (e.g. menstruation) or rabbinic (e.g. finding a stain) in origin6Mehaber and Tzemach Tzedek 195:14..

Likewise, the laws of separation are equally strict both in the five day time period (see Chapter Five) and in the seven white days7Mehaber and Tzemach Tzedek 195:14. (see Chapter Six). Furthermore, as long as the wife hasn’t immersed in a mikveh, all of the laws of separation must be followed, even if many years8Mehaber 195:1. Be’er Eliyahu on Sugah Beshoshonim 1:1. have gone by since her last menstrual cycle.

The wife follows the laws of separation from the moment she realizes her change of status; her husband starts from the moment she notifies him, either explicitly, or through her actions9Sugah Beshoshonim 2:19..

A couple, while in the company of other people, is not permitted to be lenient in the laws of separation for the sake of keeping the wife’s niddah state a secret10Shach 195:17, Igros Moshe Yoreh Deah (2) section 77..

Even if a couple is sure that leniency in these matters will not create any additional feelings of intimacy they are, nevertheless, forbidden to be lenient11Poskim, see Sefer Hachinuch Mitzvah 188..

It is praiseworthy to be extra cautious in matters of separation12Tzemach Tzedek 195:5, Sidrei Tahara 195:4.. It is, however, not recommended to create new stringencies which are not already recorded in codes of Jewish law13Tahara Kehalacha 14:3..

The husband may not act leniently in any of these laws in order to persuade his wife to immerse in the mikveh, for example if there is tension or disharmony between them14Poskim, see Shach 195:17..

Physical Contact

The following actions are forbidden during this time of separation:

  • Touching, whether through direct body contact15Rambam Sefer Hamitzvos Loh Ta'aseh 353 brought to halacha by Mehaber Even Haezer 20:1. See also Sugah Beshoshonim 3:1 and Shach 195:20. or through the clothing16Sugah Beshoshonim 3:2..
  • Insignificant contact, even touching with the little finger17Mehaber and Tzemach Tzedek 195:2.. If this is done in an affectionate way it is forbidden by the Torah18Mehaber Even Haezer 20:1. See Shach Yoreh Deah 195:20 - brings the respective opinions of the Rambam and the Ramban..

  • Holding an umbrella for each other while walking, lest they inadvertently come to touch, unless the situation demands it20Sugah Beshoshonim 3:11, Shiurei Shevet HaLevy on 195:2..   • Passing an object to each other, even a long object, (where the chance of inadvertently coming into contact is unlikely)22Tzemach Tzedek 195:2..
  • Passing an animate object which can carry itself, for example a child or baby23Aruch Hashulchan, Tahara Kehalacha 14:11..
  • To remove or place an object from a bag, while the spouse is holding it, for example a shopping bag or purse24Poskim..
  • For either to caress, kiss25Darchei Teshuva 195:11., feed, dress or give a pacifier to a baby held by the other26Sugah Beshoshonim 21:6..
  • Blowing lint off each other’s clothing27Tzemach Tzedek 195:2, Pischei Teshuva 195:4..
  • Fanning one another28Poskim..
  • Sitting on a sofa, bench, car back seat or swing when they can feel one another’s body weight is forbidden.   • Lifting, carrying, pushing or pulling even a light object together, even when other people are assisting31Bodei Hashulchan 195:26..
  • Pushing a baby carriage together.
  • Walking a child, each parent holding one of the child’s hands, if s/he is too young to walk unassisted. However if s/he can walk unassisted, they are permitted32Bodei Hashulchan 195:26..
  • If the husband is blind they are permitted to hold opposite ends of a common object, such as a stick, in order for her to lead him in the street.

  • When the wife is in bed, he may place the baby directly on her bed without touching her blanket or clothing.
  • It is permissible to simultaneously hold or touch a non-moving object, such as the dinner table34Poskim, Bodei Hashulchan 195:26..

Touching Through Clothing

The prohibition of touching includes not touching one another’s clothing while it is being worn. This includes parts of the garments which extend from the body, such as a long dress35Darchei Teshuva 195:6, Pischei Teshuva 195:3..

They should also be careful that their clothes don’t touch36Aruch Hashulchan, see Bodei Hashulchan 195:15.. Similarly, the blankets, covering them (which halachically are likened to clothing) may not come into contact37Darchei Teshuva 195:33..

It is permissible however, for the husband to touch his wife’s clothing while she is not wearing it or her bed sheets, even if they are soiled with niddah blood38Taz 195:6..

Beds—distance/making/sitting

Preparing Water

In his presence, she may not bring even cold water, for him to wash his hands, face, or feet54Mehaber 195:12.. While not in his presence, or if he turns his head55Bodei Hashulchan 195:154, poskim., this is allowed56Bach, poskim..

Throwing

The following activities are forbidden during this time:

  • Tossing an object one to the other
  • Tossing an object up for the other person to snatch out of the air59Tzemach Tzedek 195:2.

Eating Together

Since eating together can lead to intimacy, the Talmudic sages required some sort of reminder of the wife’s niddah state60Even when one of them is eating a food which the other cannot due to illness, Sugah Beshoshonim 4:26.. This is to ensure that they don’t come to share each other’s food directly, which could lead to untimely and unwanted affectionate physical contact.

There are two types of reminders: placing an object not usually on the table between them while eating and using different placemats.

The laws governing eating apply even:

  • When eating a snack together61Sugah Beshoshonim 4:24, Bodei Hashulchan 195:32. or sitting together just to drink62Shiurei Shevet HaLevy on Mehaber 195:3. Tahara Kehalacha 14:50, see discussion in Sugah Beshoshonim 5:1 and Be’er Eliyahu 5:1. Bodei Hashulchan 195:32.
  • When eating with other people63Tzemach Tzedek 195:3. Bodei Hashulchan 195:34.
  • When other people who are present might notice thereby compromising their privacy in this matter64Shach 195:17.
  • If she entered the niddah state during a meal, the couple must begin acting appropriately65Sugah Beshoshonim 4:33. Note: “If she became niddah during the meal, the husband is permitted to finish,” (Pischei Teshuva 195:7) this refers to consuming her leftover food, not a reminder between them.

A Reminder Between Them

When a reminder is required:

  • A reminder is required even when sitting so far from each other at the same table that one cannot reach into the other’s plate66See Tahara Kehalacha 14:61, “In an urgent situation, they may sit at the same table at a distance which won’t allow them to come into contact and someone sits between them.” For differing opinion see Sugah Beshoshonim 4:29..
  • Although two people eating together, one eating milk and the other meat, must have a reminder on the table to guarantee they don’t share their respective foods, the husband and his niddah wife (one eating meat and the other milk) can suffice with only one reminder67Sugah Beshoshonim 4:26. This reminder suffices for the prohibition of eating milk and meat at the same table as well as the niddah situation. Shiurei Shevet HaLevy on 195:3..
  • Even when the husband and wife wouldn’t presently eat from one another’s plates due to a quarrel68Ramo and Taz 88:2, Sugah Beshoshonim 4:30..
  • Eating with a different hand than usual, however, does not allow eating together without a reminder69Sugah Beshoshonim 4:22..

Note: The wife changing her seat from the normal place when she is pure is not a sufficient reminder70Tzemach Tzedek 195:3..

A reminder is not required:

  • When one is eating and the other is just sitting71Sugah Beshoshonim 4:27.
  • When they are sitting at two separate tables

Separate Placemats as a Reminder

Eating on separate placemats, or one of them using a placemat and the other not is a sufficient reminder, as long as this is not their custom during the time when the wife is not in the niddah state82Tzemach Tzedek 195:3.. Folding the edge of the tablecloth and placing one of their plates on the table or on that folded part is a sufficient reminder83Tzemach Tzedek 195:3.. Same Plate

Eating out of the same plate is forbidden even if other people are eating from it85Sugah Beshoshonim 4:4.. This includes not eating from the same bag86Sugah Beshoshonim 4:5., even one after the other.

The prohibition of eating from the same plate applies even when eating at a meal which is served due to a mitzvah87Sugah Beshoshonim 4:12..

This prohibition of eating out of the same plate, bowl, or bag is only when the food is consumed directly after it is removed from the vessel. For example, nuts, potato chips, french fries, techina and other dips. However, it is permissible when placed on their separate plates before being consumed. Examples are a few nuts, a few potato chips, pieces of meat, potatoes, salads, soups88Sugah Beshoshonim 4:6.. The fact that it is placed on their separate plates itself is considered a reminder.

Tasting food in a pot to see if it requires more salt or spices is not considered as eating from the same pot89Sugah Beshoshonim 4:11..

Cutting pieces off of a loaf of bread should be done as follows: they should each cut a piece off of the loaf and place it on their respective plates and then eat it from there. They should not cut off pieces and eat them directly90Sugah Beshoshonim 4:16..

The same should be done while eating a piece of watermelon91Sugah Beshoshonim 4:16.—placing the cut pieces on the table in front of them92Sugah Beshoshonim 4:18..

Eating out of separate compartments in the same serving tray, for example those used on airlines, is forbidden93Sugah Beshoshonim 4:4. Separation would not help.. However, if the food is in different containers sitting in the same serving tray, it is permissible, as long as a reminder is placed between them94Sugah Beshoshonim, Be’er Eliyahu 4:4. Consuming the Wife’s Leftover Food or Drink

The husband is permitted to consume the wife’s leftover food or drink if at least one of the following conditions exists:

  • She has left the room101Ramo 195:4.. He is allowed to ask her to leave the room for this purpose102Bodei Hashulchan 195:74..
  • He didn’t know she ate or drank from the food or drink. In such a case, she is not required to tell him103Tzemach Tzedek 195:4..
  • The food or drink was transferred104This only allows food or drink which normally require a plate or cup, for example liquids, cooked vegetables, etc., but not, for example cupcakes and similar foods, Sugah Beshoshonim 6:9. into another plate or cup105This can be done intentionally for this purpose, Sugah Beshoshonim 6:10.   • Someone else ate or drank from it between the wife and the husband107Tzemach Tzedek 195:4..

The above prohibitions only apply to food and drink, not other things like toothpaste108Bodei Hashulchan 195:51., soap, shoe polish, and shampoo109Pischei Teshuva 195:6, poskim..

If the wife cut a piece off a loaf of bread the husband is permitted to eat the remainder. However, he is forbidden to finish her slice of bread or individual roll113Igros Moshe Yoreh Deah (1) section 92. Bodei Hashulchan 195:51. Gefen Porioh 2:8..

Jesting and Speaking

They must eliminate conversation or behavior suggestive of intimacy.

Conversation dealing with daily affairs, such as running the household, is permitted139Tzemach Tzedek 195:1.. They may not study together from the same book, perchance they come to touch140Shiurei Shevet HaLevy on 195:2..

Traveling

They are not permitted to travel together, even when accompanied by other people141Bodei Hashulchan 195:94., if the purpose of the trip is the enjoyment of the excursion itself. Travelling somewhere for another purpose (for example to visit friends or relatives142Sugah Beshoshonim 22:4.) is permitted143Ramo 195:5..

If traveling on a bus, they may not sit in adjoining seats. This is to avoid contact between them or their clothing on unexpected sharp turns.

If travelling on a plane with adjoining seats, extreme caution must be exercised against contact between them or their clothing. He is permitted to bring flowers home in honor of Shabbos151Shiurei Shevet HaLevy on 195:2..

Gazing at the Wife

Looking152Intentional gazing is certainly forbidden. Some poskim suggest that the prohibition might extend to unintentional as well, see Bodei Hashulchan on Mehaber 195:7 in Beorim. The distinction, however is very fine. “Regarding those places on her body which are normally kept covered, although it may well be that there is a distinction between inadvertently seeing them and intentionally gazing at them, nonetheless this distinction is hard to make. The difference is hair-splitting fine,” Shiurei Shevet HaLevy on 195:7. at places on the wife’s body which are normally covered is forbidden153Mehaber 195:7. even when looking through thin garments, even in a mirror154Poskim, see also Mehaber Orach Chaim 75:5. Igros Moshe Yoreh Deah (2) section 75..

Those parts of the body which are normally covered are155See Orach Chaim 75:1.:

  • all the hair on her head156Shiurei Shevet HaLevy on Mehaber 195:7. See Bodei Hashulchan 195:114 and Beorim..
  • from the neck to just below the elbow. From there to the wrists depends on local custom157Tahara Kehalacha 14:21..
  • the body from the neck to the bottom of the feet and including the bottom of the feet158Mehaber 195:7..

During this time, the wife should be careful about walking in the home with her legs exposed, i.e. without socks, or sleeping with her bare legs exposed at night, and should exercise modesty while nursing the baby159Tahara Kehalacha 14:22, Kvuda Bas Melech 2:1..

The husband should minimize his gazing at permissible places if this creates feelings of intimacy160Ramo and Tzemach Tzedek 195:7..

In order for the wife to maintain her personal beauty and dignity, the sages permitted her to use makeup sparingly during this time161Mehaber 195:9.. However, she should minimize this as much as possible162Tzemach Tzedek 195:9..

Sickness

If the husband is ill she is allowed to help him recline and get up, to pass things directly to him, to tie and untie his shoe laces and to pour his drink for him. This is on condition that no one else is available to do so163Tzemach Tzedek 195:15..

If possible, she should perform these with a change from the norm (shinui). Likewise, if possible, she should attempt to refrain from washing him and making his bed in his presence164Tzemach Tzedek 195:15..

If she is sick, he is forbidden to touch her unless there is no one else available to do so and she needs it urgently165His assisting her is more strict, since when he is ill his yetzer is more dormant, Tzemach Tzedek 196:16..
